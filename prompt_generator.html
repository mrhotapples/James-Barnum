The One-Hour Hero

One hour of talking. Zero scripts. Here’s the exact workflow: prep your talking points, record in one sitting with whatever camera you already own, and hand off footage an editor can turn into polished hero videos and short clips.

Straight up: you don’t need a script.

You need a handful of good points and the willingness to say them out loud.

Scripts have a problem. The moment you read one on camera, you sound like you’re reading one, and people can tell in about two seconds. What they connect with is you thinking out loud about something you know cold.

So the plan isn’t to write a perfect talk. It’s to know your points, hit record, and talk. You’ll ramble. You’ll repeat yourself. That’s fine, because that’s what an editor is for. Your only job is to hand over a lot of real, usable raw material.

A hero video is one clear, grounded video where you explain one idea to one person. The short clips come out of it. Here’s the whole workflow:

  1. Prep (about 10 minutes): Turn your topic into a one-page cheat sheet of talking points and shot ideas with the tool below.

  2. Set up (about 15 minutes): Prop your phone, find the light, get the room quiet.

  3. Record (about an hour): Talk through your points with pauses between them, and a few extra takes of the opening and the close.

  4. Hand off: Send the raw file. Cutting, audio cleanup, pacing, and B-roll are the editor’s problem, not yours.

Step 1: Prep your talking points

Pick one topic. If you can’t decide, use the question your clients ask you over and over. You’ve answered it a hundred times, so you’ll have plenty to say, and your future clients are asking it too.

Then fill in the blanks below. The more of you that goes into the blanks (your opinions, your stories, the things you refuse to say), the less generic the cheat sheet will be. This tool organizes what’s already in your head. It can’t invent it for you.

Before you hit record, make it yours.

  1. Cross out anything that doesn’t sound like you.
  2. Rewrite the bullets in your own words. If you’d never say it out loud, don’t keep it.
  3. Add a story or example of your own to every point you can.
  4. Trim it until it fits on one screen. You’ll glance at it between takes, not read from it.
  5. Keep the shot ideas you’ll actually use and ignore the rest.

This is where the AI stops. It organized your thoughts. The voice, the delivery, and the face are all yours.

Step 2: Set up with what you already have.

Don’t buy anything yet. Your phone is plenty. Get the picture decent and the sound clean, and don’t sweat the rest.

What the camera sees

  • Use the back cameraIf you can prop the phone up, it’s usually sharper than the selfie camera. Wipe the lens first. It’s almost always smudged.
  • Film horizontalAn editor can crop a horizontal video into a vertical clip, but can’t turn a vertical video into a good widescreen one. Leave a little room around you.
  • Face the lightPut a window in front of you or beside you, never behind you. Behind means silhouette.
  • Use your real settingTidy your actual workspace, but don’t blur it or fake it. Real beats sterile.
  • Eye levelCamera at eye level, you slightly off-center, not looking down into the lens.

What the mic hears

  • Pick a quiet roomTurn off fans and anything humming. Soft things like couches, curtains, and rugs soak up echo. Bare walls bounce it around.
  • Silence the phoneDo Not Disturb on. One buzz can ruin a great take.
  • Get the mic closeDistance is the biggest factor in how good audio sounds. An inexpensive clip-on mic is the best money you can spend. Wired earbuds with a mic can work in a pinch.
  • Test for 10 secondsRecord a few words, put on headphones, and listen. Thirty seconds now saves you a ruined hour later.

About those shot ideas

Your cheat sheet suggests a shot for each point: close-up, medium, wide, a cutaway. With one camera you can’t switch lenses mid-take, but you can still use them. Move the phone closer for your emphatic points and back for the big picture. The editor cuts between the angles so the video doesn’t feel like one static shot.

Cutaways and B-roll are the editor’s job, so you don’t need to film them. Just treat those suggestions as ideas for what the video could look like.

Step 3: Record.

One hour. One sitting. Talk to one person.

  1. Do a throwaway take first. The first five minutes are always stiff. Warm up on something low-stakes and don’t count it.

  2. Talk to one person. Picture the client who asks your question and answer them, not “the camera.”

  3. Pause between points. When you finish a point, stay quiet for three seconds, glance at the cheat sheet, then go on. Those silences are clean cut points for the editor.

  4. Messed up? Don’t restart. Take a breath and say the line again from the top of the sentence. Keep going. The editor will cut the mistake.

  5. Do the opening and the close two or three times. The first and last fifteen seconds matter most, so give the editor options.

  6. Keep your energy up. Stand up, or sit tall. If you tend to go flat, standing helps. Take a break every twenty minutes or so. Several files are fine.

Step 4: Hand it off.

Send the raw files exactly as they came off the phone. Don’t trim them, don’t compress them, and don’t “clean them up first.” The messy parts are the editor’s job, and the originals hold the best quality.

If you remember a part that went especially well, jot down the rough time and send it along. It helps, but it’s not required.

From there, the strongest segments get pulled, the audio gets cleaned, the pacing gets tightened, and relevant B-roll gets layered in. What comes back are finished hero videos and short clips, ready to post.

The one-page checklist

  • Topic picked (the question clients ask most is a great one)
  • Cheat sheet generated, then edited until it sounds like me
  • Phone propped at eye level, landscape, lens wiped
  • Facing the light, with the window in front or beside me
  • Quiet room, Do Not Disturb on
  • 10-second test recorded and played back with headphones
  • Throwaway take done
  • Pausing three seconds between points
  • Extra takes of the opening and the close
  • Raw files sent untouched
